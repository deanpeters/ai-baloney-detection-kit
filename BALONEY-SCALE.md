# The Baloney Scale

The Baloney Scale describes the **level of earned confidence** in an AI claim, concept, idea, or product.

It is intentionally not binary. Read the numbers as a **baloney level**: 1 is the least baloney, 5 is the most.

The Scale is a rating of where a claim sits now. For the path a claim travels to get to Real Deal, see [BALONEY-CURVE.md](BALONEY-CURVE.md).

Real product decisions rarely are.

---

## 1. Real Deal

### *Slice it up. Put it on a hoagie. Good to go.*

Characteristics:

- receipts check out
- authoritative sources are used where appropriate
- fact, inference, assumption, and best guess are clearly separated
- context is sufficient for the decision
- information is fresh enough
- meaningful validation of all four risks has occurred: valuable, usable, viable, and feasible
- important risks and constraints are visible

### Product move

**Pursue** at the next sensible level of investment.

Real Deal is the Scale's name for **Safe to Serve**: this bet, for these people, solving this problem, under these conditions, has earned the right to move forward.

Real Deal does not mean risk-free.

It means the evidence has earned forward motion.

---

## 2. Mostly Meat

### *Probably edible. Pick out the pimentos.*

Characteristics:

- core idea appears sound
- evidence is reasonably good
- some assumptions remain
- a few sources, gaps, or experiments need cleaning up
- uncertainty is visible rather than hidden

### Product move

**Pursue carefully** or run a targeted experiment before making a larger bet.

Do not throw away the sandwich because of a few pimentos.

Pick them out.

---

## 3. Real Imitation Baloney

### *Looks like the real thing. Tastes vaguely like the real thing. Still junk food.*

Characteristics:

- highly plausible story
- some real evidence
- some marketing theater
- important claims depend on weak inference or assumptions
- context may be incomplete
- validation is partial, indirect, or overly dependent on demos and benchmarks

### Product move

**Pivot or validate harder.**

This is the dangerous middle because it can look convincing enough to fund.

---

## 4. Mystery Meat

### *Nobody knows exactly what went into this.*

Characteristics:

- sources are unclear or weak
- repeated claims have lost provenance
- assumptions masquerade as facts
- context is missing
- freshness is uncertain
- metrics lack baselines or denominators
- validation consists mainly of enthusiasm, analogy, or somebody else's case study

### Product move

**Stop and investigate.**

Do not increase investment until the ingredients are known.

---

## 5. Total Crapola

### *Put it in the garden. It's fertilizer now.*

Characteristics:

- unsupported claims
- fabricated or untraceable evidence
- stale or irrelevant data
- missing customer/problem context
- solution-first thinking
- magical economics
- benchmark theater
- no meaningful validation
- certainty wildly exceeds evidence

### Product move

**Punt.**

At least until somebody brings back evidence instead of fertilizer.

---

# Rate the Checks, Not Just the Idea

The most useful application is not one overall score.

Rate each part of the kit independently:

| Check | Example Rating |
|---|---|
| Receipts | Real Deal |
| Labels | Mostly Meat |
| Context | Mystery Meat |
| Sell-By | Real Imitation Baloney |
| Taste | Total Crapola |

Then summarize:

- where confidence is earned,
- where baloney entered the system,
- what evidence would change the rating,
- and what the next smallest sensible move should be.

The scale should guide decisions, not decorate reports.
