# Weak Example: Pursue, Pending Validation

**Anti-pattern. Synthetic.** The AI haiku feature from [funnel case 03](../../../evals/loop-cases/loop-03-nobody-would-buy-it.yaml). It sounds decisive, and it is the wrong call.

**Recommendation: PURSUE (pending validation).**

The haiku summaries are technically solid (no errors in a month), cheap to run ($0.002 per report), and popular in trials (31% tried them). Willingness to pay is lower than hoped at 6%, but that should improve as we refine the haiku quality and market the feature. Overall the bet is about 75% de-risked. Recommend we pursue the paid tier while continuing to validate demand.

**Owner:** Product. **Next step:** launch the paid tier in Q1.

---

## Why this is weak

- **"Pursue, pending validation" is a contradiction.** Pursue means so much proof that we go forward as planned. Pending validation means we don't have it.
- **It averaged the grid.** "75% de-risked" lets three earned squares paper over a failed Valuable. The weakest square decides.
- **Valuable failed, not missing.** 0 of 40 trial accounts kept it on, and finance buyers asked to turn it off. They tried it; they wouldn't buy it. That is the orange not being worth the squeeze: **Punt** the paid tier.
- **"Better haiku" fixes the wrong square.** Quality is a Usable lever; nobody said the haiku were bad, they said they wouldn't pay.
- **"Tried it" is not "trust and adopt."** 4% used it more than twice.
- **Viable is one-sided.** Cheap for us to run isn't a reason for anyone to pay.
- **No why, no reopen condition, no real owner role.** "Product" owns everything; nobody owns this call.

The right call: **Punt** the paid tier, say no plainly, and name what would reopen it (a buyer segment that asks for it unprompted).
