# Weak Example: Everyone Else Is Doing It

**Anti-pattern. Synthetic.** The same Tallyhouse help-assistant bet as the worked example. Every source is real and fresh; the map is still wrong.

| Evidence | Relevant? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Brightfold cut tickets 40% | ✓ Highly relevant | Direct competitor, same category |
| Analyst: SMBs prefer self-service | ✓ Relevant | Confirms market demand |
| Three vendors launched AI help this year | ✓ Relevant | Industry trend |
| Our enterprise pilot: tickets down 18% | ✓ Relevant | Supports the case |

**Rating:** Real Deal. Four relevant sources agree. **Exit:** Pass to Taste. Expect a 40% reduction.

---

## Why this is weak

- **No kind of relevance is named.** Direct, adjacent, and analog are all flattened into a checkmark, so a competitor's blog post counts as much as our own telemetry.
- **No mechanisms, no differences.** Brightfold's users are accountants with three times the help content. Nobody asked whether that matters.
- **"Others launched it" is not demand.** Three vendor launches say something about vendors, not about our owners.
- **The pantry was never opened.** Our ticket export, which shows half the volume sits on two screens, and the cost per conversation weren't consulted.
- **It imported a number.** "Expect a 40% reduction" borrows the conclusion along with the story.

The right map rates the competitor as a weak analog, keeps its mechanism, drops its number, and lets our own tickets narrow the bet.
