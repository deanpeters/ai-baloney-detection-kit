## 3. Tier Findings

**Chaos: Is it real, and is it still true?**  
Origins collapsed: most launch-week claims come from Beigeworks-controlled material: press release, sales deck, LinkedIn post, all-hands slide, CEO keynote, product page, pricing page, release notes, and pilot readout. They are not independent just because they appear in different formats.

Stale items and refresh: C3 uses last year’s retired model and must be refreshed before it can support today’s decision.

Receipts rating: **Mystery Meat**. Several big claims lack named sources, methods, dates, or independent support.  
Sell-By rating: **Mystery Meat**. One explicit stale benchmark; most claims have unclear observation dates.

**Confusion: What do we actually know, and does it apply to us?**  
Load-bearing assumptions: “customers love it,” “entire discovery process,” “pay for itself,” and “100% adoption day one.”  
Decisive context gaps: target users, workflows, baselines, adoption behavior, customer ROI, Beigeworks cost, ownership when wrong, and quality/rework impact.

Labels rating: **Real Imitation Baloney**. Some claims are meaningful once narrowed, but many blend marketing category claims with workflow claims.  
Context rating: **Mystery Meat**. C8 has a visible economic model, but its adoption assumption is not credible for the decision.

**Crapola: Would reality disagree?**  
Squares tested or to test: C9 needs customer-data testing; C10 has pilot evidence for a bounded duplicate-ticket triage workflow.  
Feasible-only flags: C9. A working demo is not enough to auto-close customer tickets without human review.  
Taste rating: **Mostly Meat** for C10’s bounded pilot claim; **Mystery Meat** for C9’s no-human-review release claim.

Weakest consequential finding: **C9 at Crapola / Feasible-only flag.** A demo-backed agent that auto-closes tickets with no human review could instantiate confidence faster than evidence, creating customer trust, data, and workflow risk.

Scope not earned: “entire discovery process,” no-human-review auto-close, broad product platform claims, annual contract, or public “customers love it / 40% / 10x” claims.

Suggested owner role: product owner for backlog operations, with customer success and data/AI risk review.

By when: before any broader launch, annual commitment, or removal of human approval.

What would change the call: inspected logs contradict the time savings; false-close rate exceeds the prewritten threshold; users reject the review burden; economics fail after pricing/support cost is included; model/version changes.

Where the claim goes next: **Truth, scoped to this bet only**: three named customer teams, duplicate-ticket triage, about 6 to 1 hours/week, four-week pilot, human approval for every close. Recheck on expansion, automation level change, model retirement, or contract/rollout decision.
