## 4. Alternatives worth comparing

| Alternative | Why it might win |
|---|---|
| Remove or simplify the work | If the real problem is a narrow reconciliation rule or repeated invoice pattern, simpler workflow changes may save time without per-run model cost. |
| Deterministic rule / manual service | For repeat vendors, exact-match thresholds, known exceptions, or amount/date tolerances, deterministic matching may be cheaper and more auditable. |
| Improve today’s workaround | If users already export/import spreadsheets or use manual review queues, improving import, matching hints, or exception triage may produce much of the benefit with lower cost. |
| Usage-based or tiered pricing | Better matches cost to consumption and avoids hiding the worst segment inside the median. |
| Capped flat plan | Preserves simple pricing but limits exposure: e.g., included runs per seat, then overage or degraded/manual workflow. |

## 10. Smallest context action

Run a pricing-and-workflow inspection before launch:

1. Segment beta users by run volume: median, top quartile, and highest users.
2. For each segment, calculate gross contribution using actual runs, current model cost, support cost, and any known non-model costs.
3. Observe 3 to 5 real reconciliation workflows from beta accounts and record: who starts the agent, who reviews output, what errors occur, who fixes them, and how much review/exception time remains.
4. Ask finance/vendor owner for the decision date by which Q1 2027 provider pricing must be known or hedged.
5. Compare three pricing shapes: flat with cap, tiered usage, and usage-based add-on.
