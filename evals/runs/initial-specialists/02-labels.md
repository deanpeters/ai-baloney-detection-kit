# Label the Lunch Meat — adoption and economics

As of 2026-10-10. DUMP / Product Coach, unattended. Decision: fund a small pricing test for a draft-only research assistant. Only the fictional supplied fixture was assessed; no independent verification or browsing. Sources below refer to `evals/cases/02-mixed-claims.md`.

The usage record supports first use, not willingness to pay or doubled productivity. The pricing test may be worth running precisely because those claims remain unearned.

**Label the Lunch Meat: 5 — Total Crapola for the sponsor memo's labeling discipline.** L3, L7 and L8 promote missing support to certainty. L4–L6 are useful transparent planning ingredients once kept conditional. This rating concerns claim handling, not the assistant's eventual value or anyone's intent.

The decisive corrections are: replace “adoption proves willingness to pay” with “8 of 20 invitees tried it; payment and continued use remain unmeasured”; retain $10,000 as conditional gross revenue; remove the margin and productivity promises until costs and outcome data exist. If price or paying-account count changes, revisit L6 and any dependent profitability claims.

**Pursue a small pricing test only, as a provisional recommendation.** Suggested owner: Product Manager, with finance support; actual owner and spending approval missing. Test a real price choice or purchase commitment where authorized, track continued use and successful tasks, and capture review plus operating costs. Customer demand, contribution margin and productivity improvement remain unvalidated. This assessment supplies no funding approval. Better rating requires visibly qualified claims and stronger receipts, not turning every hypothesis into FACT.
