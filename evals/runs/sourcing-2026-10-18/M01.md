**Step 2, Receipts: Sifted out.**

- **The number has no method.** The 40% comes from an interested party's "internal study of 12 users" with no methodology. That meets the sift-out rule for an outcome number offered as evidence without a method. It is a claim about what Brisket says, not proof of what happened.
- **Scope inflation along the chain.** S3 says "faster *spec drafting*." S1's headline says "*productivity* gains." Drafting is one slice of PM work, and the share of a PM's week it takes is missing.
- **The benchmark is for a superseded model.** S3 used Q-2026-03, and S6 (2026-09-30) says the product now runs on Q-2026-09. The result describes a version you wouldn't be buying. I'm noting this here, but the Sell-By step was not run on this claim. If #1 returns, this is a Send-back at Sell-By.

It is small, self-reported, and says nothing about whether SpecPilot fixes it. It is the start of a baseline, not support for the 40%.
- **Receipts rating: Total Crapola for the 40% productivity claim as stated.** Confidence (three articles, a 12-month commitment) far exceeds support (one unexplained n=12 vendor figure on an older model). This does *not* show the number is false.
