## 5. Antagonist's case

The strongest alternative explanation is that "confidence" is the output wearing a feeling, as the contract puts it. Here is how this pitch could succeed on paper and fail in the world:

1. **Confidence can rise while nothing improves.** S1 shows exactly that. Five of six people felt more confident about something they never used.
2. **It can rise while things get worse.** An AI interface that sounds sure of itself can raise confidence while accountants check less. In accounting, misplaced confidence is the failure mode, not the goal.
3. **The 50% may be borrowed.** "Up to 50%" in a blog is a ceiling from an unnamed survey. Where a pitch's number matches the headline of a source with no method, ask whether the number came from the sponsor's analysis or from the headline. I can't tell from the fixture, and I'm not asserting it.
4. **No baseline means no way to lose.** With no measure today (S3), the initiative can't miss its target. That is a property of an unfalsifiable pitch, not a strength.
5. **A mockup demo proves nothing about work.** If the aim is "easier," the next honest question is easier at what, for whom. S4 says the slide answers neither.

I'm arguing against the pitch's wording. The sponsor may well have a real problem in mind, and the shaped statement above is an attempt to give it a testable form.
